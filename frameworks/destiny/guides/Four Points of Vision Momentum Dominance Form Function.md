# Four Points of Vision: Momentum, Dominance, Form, Function

**Source:** Lesson 2 — The Vision

Break your vision into four separate points to examine its shape and implications.

## 1. The Momentum

Time is required for reflection on what this vision means for your personal life and the implications for the latter years. Examine your strengths, weaknesses, and abilities. As thoughts and desires gain momentum, there will be conflicting anguish if you are serious. Try to imagine going through the vision in your mind, transporting yourself through the corridors of time. Avoid sharing your progress with others during this embryo stage; negative comment can destroy a good idea. Observe times of incubation, add inspirational material, and avoid the despondency of weariness.

## 2. The Dominance

To foster a vision, there must be one dominant desire or requirement. Assess dominance by comparing its value with the life that will be expended in its achievement. Measure in terms of capacity, impact, longevity, and value in exchange for your life.

**Questions for your dominant vision:**
- Does it fit your value system, or will you have to make adjustments?
- Can you lead a full and complete family life within a tightly whole frame?
- Are you prepared to persevere and complete it without support or external encouragement?
- Are you willing to develop your intellectual and other capacities to study what is required?
- What level of personal pain, pressure, and anguish can you endure?
- How will you maintain balance and perspective against negative influences?

## 3. The Form

Create outlines of what your vision really is. Put pen to paper with a general outline; when finished, it represents your life and everything it stands for. Keep the description simple without embellishments—more like a photograph than an abstract painting. Avoid psychological phrases and flowery words without clear descriptions.

## 4. The Function

The function must have as its overriding passion a compulsion and an urgency factor. The vision will never be realized if it has an escape clause. Your capacity to endure and live with your vision will at times seem more than you can bear—but there are moments of incredible excitement as you see it becoming living reality.

*If the vision is to develop fully, it will enter your mind and possess your spirit as a relentless companion. In moments of rest it will arrest your being and propel you forward, reminding you of the picture you saw of yourself as you were meant to be.*
