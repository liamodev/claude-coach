# Concentrate on Essentials

**Source:** Lesson 2 — The Vision

One of the ways you can take control of your time is by adopting an uncluttered lifestyle. Get rid of excess baggage, incidentals, and encumbrances that limit effectiveness and only burn up time and energy.

## Principles

- **Share the excesses** — Don't make anything complicated. Make things simple and effective.
- **Use a direct approach** — Speak directly; say yes or no on the spot without going through intermediaries.
- **Keep it simple** — Accounts up to date, no mail left unanswered, streamlined systems.
- **Recognize where you are vulnerable** — Do an occasional "commando raid": ask how you would retain and improve your lifestyle if war, financial collapse, fire, illness, bereavement, embezzlement, or market collapse occurred.

## Key Questions

1. If you had to start again, what would your minimum requirements be? How does this compare with your present requirements? What action will you take and when?
2. If you had to cut costs (personal or procedures) and still maintain effectiveness, what would you cut out?
3. If you were forced to double your income without more cost to protect those you love, how would you do it?
4. If all financial arrangements were terminated immediately, how would you survive?
5. List six ways you can increase your effectiveness without additional cost (meaning less work for you).
6. For any activity involving your personal time: What are the unique linchpins? What can be on automatic control? What personal qualities are required? What inconsistencies are obvious? How much time have you allocated to family, rejuvenation, and enjoyment?

**Reserves:** Keep a reserve fund for emergencies only, separate from operating accounts. Create a small separate account for fun money so you can enjoy time off.
