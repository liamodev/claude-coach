# Formula for Being Motivated All the Time

**Source:** Lesson 6 — The Destiny

Motivation is not so much learning new principles as remembering and renewing what we have long forgotten and being stimulated to new dimensions of thought. Good motivation means moving towards a predetermined destination. It goes hand in hand with **urgency** and **dignity**. Many things motivate: threats, fear, commitment, crisis, conflict, love, anger.

## Principles

**Motivation is Deeply Personal** — Nobody can act it out for you. It determines your lifestyle, your relationships, your disciplined personality. It acts as a magnet to others. Your personality can be changed.

**Have Clearly Defined Boundaries** — You cannot be motivated if you want to do everything; be selective. Do not allow intrusions into your preferred areas. Reassess boundaries regularly for saboteurs. Boundaries are selected choices, not limitations.

**Have Long-Term Goals** — A goal for life ensures motivational permanence. Long-term goals create commitment, turn fantasy thinking into fantastic action, can reprogram your body clock, and ensure long-term satisfaction.

**How to Develop a Positive Mental Attitude** — Believe that anything could be done by somebody; why not you? Act physically motivated and you will be mentally stirred. Create a daily affirmation sheet. Develop thought displacement. Use mind talk—don't let your subconscious lead you where you do not want to go.

**Develop Deep Personal Integrity** — It is dishonest to say you will do something then not do it. Be someone people can admire. Accept and maintain personal discipline. Do not play dangerous games with your subconscious.

**Select Your Friends Carefully** — Do not try to be the top in your group; expand your relationships. Mix well in all social strata. Look for friends who are honest, have a sense of humor, are high achievers, and are generous.

**Personal Timetables** — Time does not expand. You have all the time you're going to get. Don't allow others to control your time frames. Keep a respect for your own lifespan. Time is your most valuable possession.

**Minimize People Dependency** — Operate under a time-relationship system; don't follow the crowd. Fewer people, more efficiency. Examine regularly your people quotient.

**Remain in Control All the Time** — Control means you can change any time. Control means loosening the reins and delegating with guidelines and a reporting system. Control means following your plan; setting principles, not dominating every event. Do not be manipulated by people or events.

**Accept Total Personal Responsibility** — If you cannot take personal responsibility, how can you be trusted? Total responsibility means total planning. Control means anticipating events, not explaining them after. Responsibility can mean personal confrontation.

**Make Motivation a Habit** — Move good habits into automatic control. Never let an exception occur. Be objective always; do not confuse activity with objectivity.

**Aspire to Greater Things Than Yourself** — Develop a grand magnificent obsession: of benefit to others, large in impact, of permanence. When you feel small, think big. When you feel down, get up. When you feel confused, seek clarity and a God perspective.

**Always Have a Futuristic Look** — Welcome change. Program change. Control change.

**Give Your Motivations to Others** — Be affirming. Lead, don't dominate. Teach personal principles at work.

**Be Grateful Not Critical** — There is always a bright side. You are what you have made of your life. Criticism is loveless.

**Remove Motivational Blocks Forever** — Turn problems into procedures. Have minimum routine daily procedures. Program to eliminate your biggest motivational block forever.

**Look for Mentors** — Mentors expand us; choose in different fields; deceased mentors can inspire.

**Have Built-in Personal Incentives** — Reward program even for yourself: short-term/intangibles, medium-term/pride, long-term.

## Three Simple Motivational Disciplines

1. Understand yourself. Create a list of your disciplines and the limits you place on yourself and allow others to place on you.
2. Understand that all people respond differently; appreciate the uniqueness of every human being.
3. Develop a working knowledge in the behavioral sciences to motivate both yourself and others to desirable action.

## Simple Team Motivation Principles

- Set the direction to be established
- Establish accountability
- Foster a climate of trust
- Provide financial recognition and other incentives

## Continued Team Motivation

- Get people aside; ask where they would like to improve
- Provide education and products
- Encourage personal development
- Give opportunity for corporate growth
