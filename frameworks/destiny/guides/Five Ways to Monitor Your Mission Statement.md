# Five Ways to Monitor Your Mission Statement

**Source:** Lesson 5 — The Mission (Assessing the Dynamics)

Lock in regular time to review each of the principles. When something goes wrong, go back and read the instructions. Five easy ways to monitor the continued effectiveness of your dynamic mission statement:

1. **Does everyone feel included?** Isolation for anyone with a role will create blockages. For personal goals where family and friends are involved, everyone diffident or unaware can cause the mission to flounder. In corporations, clarity of perception relating to facts needs to be assured.

2. **Has it inspired a culture?** Identity and character formed from the mission as expressed and demonstrated by management. Cross signals from senior management conflicting with the mission statement is sure death to corporate culture. Evaluate the mission according to people's value systems and their ethical commitment.

3. **Do outsiders clearly understand it?** Listen to your customers or supporters—they mirror how they see the company. One method: disguise your voice and phone your own company seeking service to get direct insight into how staff understand the mission. Descriptive responses from others provide a guide to its perceived nature.

4. **Have all the distinctives been covered?** Feedback by way of difficulties caused by omissions can identify overlooked distinctives. Use caution making changes; if you insert a distinction that is not long-term, you may create more confusion than confidence. Test and try alterations. Only after absolute assessment should you make any change. Multiple changes suggest a vacillating program.

5. **What is its relationship to the productive output?** Continually ask: Is the mission relating to our productive output in a measurable way? If not, look first at adherence of the mission statement, then the vehicle, then the goal.

**Final advice:** Sloganize some parts of your mission statement in discussion, correspondence, advertising, and speeches to declare your direction. Example: "New ways to new energy for a new millennium."

**The question:** As you write your mission statement to reach your goal and finally your destiny—are you going to ask "What then? What then?" If you are, your destiny goal is too small.
