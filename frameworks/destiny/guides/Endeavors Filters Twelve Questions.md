# Endeavors Filters: Twelve Questions

**Source:** Lesson 3 — The Commitment (Life Commitment Thinking)

Whatever your life commitment is, ask these principal questions in respect to all of your endeavors.

1. **What is the measured end result required?** Demand a descriptive objective. The clearer the description and the more accurate the measurement, the easier it becomes to assess the equipment to get the job done. Avoid abstract terms like "big," "small," or "as soon as possible." Write a clear, simple description of what is finally expected.

2. **Are there any checkpoints that facilitate early correction of mistakes?** Take an imaginary walk through the process; create short-term holding patterns for inspection and perspective. Create guardian rules to protect the ground already taken. Correct problems early and fix or remove them permanently.

3. **Is the path clear and direct?** Guard against parochial interest or ego diverting you. Each misdirection weakens the total substance of your efforts. Keep to clear, concise directions that provide a straight intelligent path.

4. **Is there a real or accepted sacrifice to make?** Only you can assess a sacrifice that fits your value system. If there is no sacrifice to make, your vision may be too small. Sacrifice indicates the value you have placed on the project.

5. **Does the value of the objective reflect the pain of commitment?** Value to one individual is different from value to another. Consider voluntary vs. involuntary pain. The only measure of real value is permanence.

6. **Will it provide a definite solution to a definite need?** Avoid consuming activities that lead nowhere. Don't fantasize a solution to a disappearing or obsolete need. If you want to create a solution, make it permanent.

7. **Can you give it your best without reservation?** Giving your best includes time, talent, and effort. Are you prepared to trade your life for your vision? Any hesitation could indicate fear—find out what.

8. **Do you have a better plan, and if you do, are you prepared to risk all in it?** Double-check your facts. Is it the best plan you can put together? Minimize risk by investigation but remember: the more you insulate yourself from risk, the more you insulate yourself against success.

9. **Can you look failure in the face and start again with vigor?** Failure can act as a bottom line barometer. At the bottom, there is only one direction: up. Failures must act as springboards to success. You only lose when you give up.

10. **Are you prepared to out-think your present capacity?** There is always a better way. Relate to the unrelatable; crisscross ideas; bounce thoughts against those who disagree. Think years in advance. Several hours of focused thought with space for reflection produce the exciting and the unexpected.

11. **Will you adopt a life commitment to always seek and find with accuracy your personal destiny?** "Seek and you shall find." Go to the core of every problem and into the treasury of every opportunity. Ask the questions; dig for reasons; seek fresh alternatives until you find the truth.

12. **Will your life create a permanent change or a benefit to someone else?** In long-term planning, ask: How many years will this last without my direct involvement? Who and how many will benefit in the long term? Be honest with yourself.
