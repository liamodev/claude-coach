# Ten Points for Corporate Mission Statement

**Source:** Lesson 5 — The Mission (Getting The Bits Together)

A simple benchmark to pull the elementary threads together in preparation for creating your corporate mission statement.

1. **Clarity of direction** — The people at the lowest level must understand clearly what is meant and how they personally fit in. Any ambiguity provides a toe-hold for misunderstanding and mistakes.

2. **Care and understanding of human nature** — People fall into four groups: Maintenance (security without responsibility), Stabilizers (medium responsibility), De-stabilizers (move for highest bidder), Growth (commitment, intelligence, hunger for responsibility). Unify them in the mission.

3. **Comprehensive study of markets and trends** — Plus culture and law when marketing internationally. Nothing continually happens in business until something is sold. Pay attention to style, obsolescence, marketing strategies, start-up and future innovative trends.

4. **Tenacity for both quality and growth** — Balanced pattern on price, quality, availability, and awareness. Quality and quantity must be best exploited. Aggressiveness plus quality from the top down.

5. **Knowledge of short- and long-term economics** — Good accountant with in-depth knowledge can clear the fiscal pathway. Principles: Maintain control; beware debt that can expand; protect gains; never risk future on loan prospects; always have protective capital reserves.

6. **Vision for new concepts and developments** — Any mission that does not seek new ways, new uses, and/or alternative products is doomed to failure. Create atmosphere of innovation; reward useful ideas.

7. **Commitment to the long-term challenge** — Corporation cannot go into neutral like an individual. Vigilance at all times. Sacred trust to employees and supporters.

8. **Ability to cope with continued criticism and loneliness** — Mission statement provides known direction and allegiance of an agreed group. Leader who sticks to principles may gain hero status. Never underestimate group power from adhering to the mission.

9. **Preparedness to give others the credit** — Team effort. Leader provides direction, motivation, plans—then gives credit to others. Best response: point to all the team players. Loyalty follows genuine appreciation.

10. **Selection of directors** — Two board levels: (1) Legal board—diversity, own successful corporations, proven track record; rarely retired (dinosaur reaction to time); not those employed by others. (2) Advisory board—no legal responsibility; can include well-known names; meaningful contribution; limited time positions. Obtain commitment to mission before appointment. Have a simple means of retiring board members who do not fit.
