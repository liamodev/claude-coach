# Describe Your Pain Transaction, Time Frame, and Specifics

**Source:** Lesson 5 — The Mission (Creating a Mission Statement with Order)

Before putting your mission statement together, clarify these three areas.

## Describe Your Pain Transaction

**Success is a willingness to bear pain** — not as a masochist or to prove you can endure, but by necessity in the process of becoming what you would like to be.

Pain comes from: failure, disappointment, embarrassment, the continuing thrust to achieve your goals (often facing setbacks and major changes), the pain of achieving success day after day as you try to remove the camouflage that conceals your key to success. Pain is not being able to get out of yourself that which you know you can produce.

Generally mankind is seduced to the comfort zone, but **victories are rarely found in the comfort zone**. Most achievers are found in the pain arena. Beware the camouflage of mediocrity.

Ask yourself: Do you buckle under stress? Do you avoid confrontation? Do you avoid discomfort to the detriment of your performance? If leadership is your aspiration, understand that conflict, opposition, competition, and difficulties will always arise. Understand the ethos of followership before you can become a good leader.

## Describe Your Time Frame

Our whole existence is based on time and timing. To create a mission statement requires a time frame to reach our destinations. Any attempt to ignore these fundamentals is folly. Plan the journey so that you can predict your performance with reliable surety. Each part of the journey is a unit of the whole and provides building blocks towards the completed construction. Sometimes when young we cut the mission to bare essentials and the final destination, when achieved, is so poorly supported it can be in danger of collapse.

## Describe Your Specifics

Clear targets need to be assessed and regarded as paramount importance for your journey. Put them in written form ready for action. Ask: Is the goal fixed or can it be moved (economic, political, seasonal)? What are the numerical measurements that make a goal complete?
