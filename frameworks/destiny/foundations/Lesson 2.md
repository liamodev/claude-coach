# Lesson Two: The Vision
  
The vision, the depth, capacity, and measurable impact of a dream.

**Biblical Base for Vision:**
Job 7:14 'Then you scare me with your dreams and terrify me with your visions.'
Habakkuk 2:2-3 'And the Lord answered me saying, write the vision and make it plain on tablets that he may run who reads it. For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it will speak and it will not lie. Though it tarries, wait for it, because it will surely come. It will not tarry. And so the vision.'
  
In this lesson, we start to clarify the picture and take the next step towards the realization of our own personal destiny of the 3rd millennium.

The dreaming, the philosophy, the writing of verse, the flights of fancy, and the flowery words of generalities must now be focused into a vision.

An ancient Greek legend speaks of the gates of dreams, one of ivory and one of horn. The dreams that are false pass through the ivory gate, and the dream that is true passes through the gate of horn. On examination, we find that the 'ivory' in Greek is and that the verb elephairomai means 'to cheat with empty hopes'. Conversely, the word for 'horn' is keras, and the verb karanoo means 'I accomplish'.
  
A true dream is focused into a clear vision with rationale and structure. A timid dream is more illusionary and fanciful, and yet as it becomes more earnest, it can at times be painful. A dream can be washed away with yet another dream, whereas a vision is indelibly etched into the very being of its recipient. A dream can be thought provoking and cause moments of concern, but a vision can haunt, disturb, and unsettle. In the biblical story of Job, during his ordeal, there were those who tried to offer him comfort and heard him responding to God with these words, you scare me with your dreams and terrify me with your visions. Through this we obtain a deeper clarification.
  
In the biblical account of Habakkuk, God gives instruction to write the vision and make it plain on tablets that he may run who reads it. Obviously, the vision has a more conscious and a more significant directional part to play and must be taken more seriously. 

A vision, of course, is a dream under conscious direction. The embryo of your own personal destiny begins to grow as your vision develops. 

To talk of a vision is a serious business. It indicates form and structure. It requires an intellectual and an emotional response or an arrogant dismissal. You have now moved from feeling and inspiration to something more tangible, and so you must examine with some serious inquiry the shape and implications of your vision if you are to consider claiming it for your life pursuit. 

To do this, let's break your vision into four separate points.

  1. The momentum
  2. The dominance
  3. The form
  4. The function
  
### 1. The Momentum

We understand that the dream idea or reflection has stimulated the mind and spirit to sustain the development of the dream to the next level. Some time is now required for reflection on what this vision really means for your personal life and the implications of what it will mean in the latter years as well as in the large portions of your life that has to be spent in its quest. It may be important to take time alone for deep thought and meditation in respect to what all this means to you. 

This is a good time to examine your strengths, weaknesses, and abilities in the light of your next step, which will take you from merely dreaming to the possibility of accurately fulfilling a vision. 

The seriousness of this crucial step must not be underestimated. As your thoughts and desires gain momentum, there will be some conflicting anguish if you are the least bit serious about the pursuit. 

Doubt and mental blocks will abound as you try to come to grips with uncertainty. Like a great sculptor creating a masterpiece, many times you will remold your thoughts only to start over once again. 

Try to imagine going through the vision in your own mind, transporting yourself mentally through the corridors of time, experiencing exhilaration and pain, coming to the final realization of your vision. You do not want to stay in the timid waters of mediocrity, but to strongly resolve to be all that God created you to be, and in this quest to learn to be patient and to grow into your vision.

As you further pursue this program, many of the missing links will mysteriously fall into place, providing the material to reach your destiny of the 3rd millennium. 

It is imperative at this time to avoid sharing your progress with others because a negative or reluctant comment can easily destroy a good idea. And at this embryo stays the creation of your vision, and ultimately your destiny of the third millennium is not ready for discussion or debate. 

During this period, try to observe times of incubation, adding good inspirational material, and avoid the despondency of weariness which tends to rob you of clarity and the depth, capacity, and measurable impact of your dream.

### 2. The Dominance

Obviously, when you dream, there are many parts that make up the whole, but also many other objects or desires or needs that are of lesser perceived value at this time. To foster a vision, there must be one dominant desire or requirement. The easiest way to assess the dominance and importance to you is to compare its value with the life that will be expended and forfeited in its achievement. 

The dominance of a vision must be measured in several ways, such as capacity, impact, longevity, and value in exchange for your life. If it does not get your full life's attention, then it will remain merely a dream, along with many others that come and go throughout a lifetime and will be useful only to relieve boredom.

There are important questions that have to be asked in respect to the forming of your dominant vision, they are:

- Does it fit in with your value system, or will you have to make adjustments?
- Can you lead a full and complete family life within a tightly whole frame?
- Are you prepared to persevere and complete it without support or an external encouragement?
- Are you willing to develop your intellectual and other capacities to study the needs required?
- What level of personal pain, pressure, and anguish can you endure?
- How will you maintain your balance and perspective against negative influences?

### 3. The Form

Now is the time to make sense of all that you have experienced by creating some outlines of what your vision really is. 

From time to time, I receive phone calls requesting an appointment from people wanting to share their dream with me. My immediate reaction is to suggest that they fax the outline of the dream to me with a clear statement of why they want me to read it. Without exception, the caller told me that it's difficult to fax and impossible to discuss it over the phone. And because of the lack of clarity, they need some input from me that could only come from discussion. I have, of course, on rare occasions, given appointments only to be always disappointed because the dreamer wanted outside outside validation of their dreams. In fact, I have never met any of them at a later date with the same dream!

A dream that needs outside validation lacks a right of ownership by the participant. But the dream that becomes a vision requires no validation from an external force because the continual presence of the vision is branded on the very soul of its possessor.

Now is the time to put pen to paper, making a general outline of your vision, bearing in mind that when it is finished, it represents your life and everything it stands for. Remember, of course, that a vision is usually a generalization of what you believe you want to achieve. The detail will come later as we deal with The Commitment, The Goal, and The Mission. 

Keep the description of your vision simple without embellishments of any kind, more like a photograph rather than an abstract painting. The use of psychological phrases and flowery words without clear descriptions have no place in this all important building block towards your destiny into the 3rd millennium.

### 4. The Function

The function must have as its overriding passion a compulsion and an urgency factor. The vision will never be realized if it has an escape clause or goes along by going along. Your capacity to endure and live with your vision, never hiding from its demanding thrust, will at times seem more than you can bear. But there are always moments of incredible excitement as you see your vision taking place, not in written or imaginary form, but in fact a living reality. Whilst you are in the midst of what may seem to be a play or a scene from a movie.

Great achievers down through the centuries have commented on some aspects of their lives where they felt they were actually walking with destiny. Others have described it as being swept along with the momentum of an earlier vision. 

*If the vision is to develop fully, it will enter your mind and possess your spirit as a relentless foreboding companion. In moments of rest and at times of casual living, it will arrest your being and propel you forward reminding you of the picture you saw of yourself as you really were meant to be.*

### 4. Apply Strong Pressure to Specific Tasks

Every process in life has waiting time, obsolete functions, errors, margins and room for improvement in various areas. Recognizing this enables the development of a no fault production line in manufacturing. 

Everything can be improved, and will continue to be improved or made obsolete with another discovery, which in turn will also be improved. This process will go on as long as mankind continues to compete.

To evaluate and concentrate on your final destination, ask yourself these questions when commencing a project:

- Am I on the right track?

- Is there a better way to do this?

- Am I going straight to the problem areas, or am I creating a process which goes around the problem?

- Am I moving at the right speed?

Build some character traits that encourage you to do things right the first time. This will prevent you from being robbed of time by avoidable repetition.

In the Bible, the book of Ecclesiastes talks about a time for every purpose under heaven. And the great writer Charles Dickens, in the opening chapter of A Tale of Two Cities, says, 'It was the best of times and it was the worst of times.' It seems that every generation wrestled with the seemingly elusive mystery of time, and every creature and plant on earth has an inbuilt time frame that governs its growth and, ultimately, its direction and expiration. 

Only human beings have the capacity and opportunity to make time work for them. We can expand it by creative thought, getting more done in less time, or lose it by ignoring the capacity given to us by God to think and create within the framework of a very generous law and with an almost unlimited capacity. 

Be creative and endeavor always to conserve and expand time. Because unlike money and other gifts of life, it is irredeemable and therefore limited. But what you do with it and the respect that you give it can expand your capacity for work and enjoyment beyond any imaginary dream.


### 5. Choose Words Carefully and Accurately to Save Time

*Get into the habit of saying things clearly once.*

A great deal of unnecessary work is caused by the need to resolve misunderstandings and to clarify instructions or comments. Poor communication can finally lead to argument, suspicion, and polarization of groups, all of which waste time. Taking charge of your time means you must aim at clarity in what you say and very often even the tone that you use. 

For specific results, use specific tools. If you have a difficult statement to make, ask yourself these questions:

1. Exactly what do I want to achieve by my statement?

2. How will the recipient react?

3. Clarify in writing, if necessary, exactly what you want to say.

4. And practice using the correct tone for what you need to say.

Use simple words with uncluttered meanings, such as no rather than I don't think so, or 03:30PM rather than mid afternoon. Be specific, clear, and avoid misunderstanding like the plague. 

In being direct, let people know your standards without malice or hardness, but with clarity. 

Do not be so afraid of confrontation that you do nothing. A situation can get out of hand just because you are concerned about hurting people or because you allow yourself to be ruled by fear of having to face a difficult person. Remember to do your thinking before you're talking. 

Consider commitments, instructions, and interpretations to writing to avoid misunderstanding. 

Most people do not respond to the first instruction or request unless they understand that it's final. Save time by getting others used to the fact that you only speak on a subject once with clarity and conviction, and then the action commences without any further signals. You'll find that your communication time will be halved and your productivity will skyrocket.

When confronting others, use a role and identity method. Separate the person from the role. I would speak to an employee along these lines: Jim, you are a very likable person, and you have many talents in areas where I do not. So as a person you are quite unique. But, Jim, there is another side that does not reflect upon your person but rather your role in this organization, and that is quite distinct from who you are. Your role in this organization is to manage our books, and in that area, your work is not measured up to the standards which we have set. You see, the difference is that you're not attacking the person but centering on the role, which, of course, is a real problem. 

Always try to employ and associate with those who are reliable.

Life is usually choices, not chances, and we inevitably get out of life that which we choose or that which come to us because we did not make a choice. 

I have found over the years that I make the choice of who I'm going to do business with and to whom I'm going to relate. If my choice is not on their agenda, then I make another choice. And so the process continues. But I do look for certain character traits that help me determine whether I will get involved with people in organizations. The most important question is, are they reliable? 

I'm sure that you expected my first choice to be honesty, but I must confess that over the years, I have met a lot of nice, honest people who were neither competent nor reliable. Imagine working with people who, because they are not reliable, continually arrive late and create chaos because they honestly make the wrong choices through incompetence. 

I avoid those who exaggerate because you are forever having to adjust everything they tell you by a percentage. I watch for early behavioral patterns that ring alarm bells, such as blaming circumstances, others, machinery, and markets for problems that could have been avoided. 

Beware of the elusive rainbow seekers who always seem to have some new scheme that will make millions or those that want you to participate with them, usually because of your reputation and finance and in that which can only be achieved with your commitment.

In other words, check to see if those that you are involved with have a track record record of reliability. i.e.:
Do they keep their word? 
Do they have a good reputation? 
Are they actually skilled in their area of involvement? 
What is their success failure ratio? 
Have their predictions in the past been accurate?

Your time is a limited investment which needs to be protected, so determine the limits of tolerable behavior. You are not responsible for the behavior of others if you have been fair in your dealings with them. If they have chosen to ignore your tolerance, you should move on and keep control of your time. 

#### Choose words carefully and accurately to save time. 
- Give clear, specific direction
- Confront problems promptly
- Think before you speak!
- Say it once
- Look for reliable associates



### 6. Time Alone and Time at Work are of Equal Value

My most important work gets done when I am alone. As I reflect, imagine, and plan, my thought process has become stimulated, and I produce.

If you are a wood cutter, you need to stop to sharpen your axe. If you are a pastor of a church, you need to revitalize your spirit. If you are an athlete, you need to relax and to rest your body. If you are a soldier, then rest and recreation times are imperative for balance and resilience. 

The leader may not need the same type or quality of solitude as a follower or those in more predictable circumstances. 

I think the reason I have continued for so long without the normal complaints of burnout and boredom is that I take respites, such as a couple of days at our country property, a day or time out at the public library to think and reflect, or an extended weekend in the outback of Australia on a horse or a camel, which allows for determined, concentrated, and creative thought.

In the biblical quotation in Proverbs chapter 11 verse 25, it says, He who refreshes others, he himself will be refreshed. And it's true. And that is why we must be involved with others but governed by a predetermined goals program. 

Be careful to treat all commitments as moral obligations, and do not commit yourself to anything you do not want to do. 

Put a measurement of time and value on everything. Remember that money is only time well spent. If you have enough time, you can always earn more money, but money cannot buy your personal time.

### Time Savers and Reminders

Listed below are some simple time savers and reminders to allow you to get more done and have time left over.

1. Check that appointments are still at the agreed time and location.
2. Write letters of clarification after negotiations.
3. Don't overlook or bypass that which you do not understand.
4. Build in financial and time reserves for all occasions that are not under your total control.
5. And remember the bluntest pencil is better than the sharpest memory (write things down!).
6. Get interruption to do important tasks.
7. Simplify everything.
8. Do things in order of importance, not in order of irritation or pressure.
9. Always keep monetary change handy in case you need to park your motor vehicle or phone from a public call box.
10. Always have cash reserve
11. Do not commit yourself in haste.
12. Learn to do more by doing less. For instance, only do that which returns measurable benefits.
13. Put a percentage measurement or value on all of your time commitments.
14. Speak clearly and precisely.
15. Confront quickly, but always leave the door open.
16. Don't do yourself what others can do better and more cheaply.
17. Control the telephone for your own benefit.
18. Make a list of business essentials and forget the gimmicks and gadgets.
19. Carry a long term planner and keep to your plan.
