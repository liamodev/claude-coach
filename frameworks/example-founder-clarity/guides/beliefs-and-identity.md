# Beliefs and Identity

## What This Guide Is For

Tactics solve tactical problems. When the same problem keeps returning despite different tactics, it's a belief or identity problem. This guide surfaces the beliefs running in the background, tests them against reality, and builds the identity required for the next level.

---

## The Belief Excavation Process

When a pattern keeps repeating, run this sequence:

**Step 1 — Name the pattern**
State it plainly: "You've been avoiding this conversation for three months. You said the same thing last time. What's actually going on?"

**Step 2 — Find the belief underneath**
Ask: "If you imagine doing the thing you're avoiding, what do you worry would happen?" The answer reveals the belief. Common ones:
- "If I charge more, clients will leave."
- "If I ask for help, they'll see I don't have it together."
- "If I put myself out there and it doesn't work, I won't recover."
- "People like me don't get to have this."

**Step 3 — Test it against reality**
Not philosophically — factually. "Is that actually true? What's the evidence for it? What's the evidence against it? Has this belief ever been wrong before?"

**Step 4 — Find the identity underneath the belief**
Beliefs come from identity. Ask: "What kind of person holds that belief? Is that who you are — or who you were?" The shift is often: "I used to be someone who needed to earn safety. I'm becoming someone who operates from strength."

**Step 5 — Build the new belief through action**
New beliefs aren't installed through affirmations — they're built through evidence. Ask: "What's one action this week that the person you're becoming would take? Not a big one. A real one."

## Identity-Level Coaching

Behavior change is temporary. Identity change is permanent.

The questions that create identity shifts:

- "Who do you need to become to achieve what you say you want?"
- "What would the version of you who has already solved this do differently?"
- "What does this win tell you about who you are — not what you can do?"
- "Is this a 'you can't do this' situation — or a 'you haven't yet' situation?"

---

## Common Limiting Beliefs in Founders

These appear constantly. Know them:

- **Worthiness blocks:** "I don't deserve this level of success yet."
- **Impostor framing:** "Eventually they'll figure out I'm not as capable as they think."
- **Scarcity defaults:** "If I win, someone else loses. If I charge more, they leave."
- **Control addiction:** "If I don't do it myself, it won't be done right."
- **Delay loops:** "I'll do X when Y is in place." (Y never fully arrives.)
- **Identity ceiling:** "People from my background don't build companies like that."

---

## Coaching Notes

- Don't argue with limiting beliefs — explore them. Curiosity, not confrontation.
- The goal is not to replace a belief with a positive one. It's to help the client see that the belief is a choice, not a fact.
- Identity shifts take time and repetition. Plant the seed, return to it.
- When they achieve something significant, always anchor it to identity: "What does this tell you about who you are?"

---

## Questions to Use in Session

- What's the story you tell yourself about why this keeps happening?
- What would you have to believe about yourself for that to be true?
- When did you first start believing that?
- What would it mean about you if you succeeded at this?
- Who are you becoming — and what does that person do differently than who you are today?
