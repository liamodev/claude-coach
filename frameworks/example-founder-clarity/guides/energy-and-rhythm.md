# Energy and Rhythm

## What This Guide Is For

A founder's energy is the company's most limited resource. When it's depleted, everything degrades — judgment, creativity, leadership, and resilience. This guide helps identify what's draining it, what restores it, and how to design a rhythm that sustains high performance.

---

## The Energy Audit

Three questions to run when the client is running on empty:

**1. What is taking energy that shouldn't be?**
Look for: tasks below their skill ceiling, decisions that should be delegated, relationships that cost more than they return, environments that create friction, unresolved conflicts that sit in the background, unfinished commitments creating mental load.

**2. What is generating energy — and are they doing enough of it?**
High performers often crowd out the very things that fuel them. What work gives them energy? Who gives them energy? Are those things protected in their schedule — or happening by accident?

**3. What is the current recovery rate?**
Are they recovering between high-demand periods — or is depletion compounding? Look for: sleep, exercise, real disconnection, relationships outside work, creative outlets.

---

## The Rhythm Design Framework

A sustainable founder rhythm has four components:

**Deep work blocks** — uninterrupted time for the highest-leverage thinking and creating. Non-negotiable. Should be scheduled before anything else.

**Shallow work windows** — email, Slack, admin, quick calls. Batched, not scattered throughout the day.

**Recovery anchors** — specific daily or weekly practices that restore energy. Not aspirational ("I should exercise more") — actual anchors in the schedule.

**Decision-free time** — time with no agenda, no productivity requirement, no optimization. The brain consolidates and creates here. Founders who eliminate this are drawing down cognitive reserves they're not replenishing.

---

## Warning Signs

Flag these when they appear:

- Working more hours, producing less
- Difficulty making decisions they would normally find easy
- Emotional reactivity that's disproportionate to the situation
- Excitement replaced by obligation
- Telling themselves they'll rest "after" a milestone that keeps moving

---

## Coaching Notes

- Burnout often presents as a tactical problem ("I need a better system") when it's actually a capacity and recovery problem.
- Founders commonly resist protecting energy because it feels selfish or unproductive. Reframe: their energy is a company resource.
- Sustainable rhythm isn't about working less — it's about ensuring the work that matters gets the best version of them.

---

## Questions to Use in Session

- What's taking the most energy right now — and does it deserve that?
- What would you protect in your schedule if you knew it made everything else better?
- When did you last feel fully recovered? What was different then?
- What are you tolerating that's quietly draining you?
- If you could design the next 90 days for maximum energy, what would change?
