### 12 Endeavors Filters

- [x] **Filter 1.** What is the measured end result required? (Write a clear, specific description.)
  > The consulting firm is a stepping stone — not the destination. The model: use consulting engagements to understand businesses deeply, transform them with AI, and gain the insider intelligence that a buyer needs. The measured end result is majority stake in a portfolio of companies with combined revenue of at least $30M/year, achieved within 4 years. Framework: PE-style business acquisition and turnaround, AI transformation as the value-creation lever, consulting as the diligence pipeline into acquisition targets.

- [x] **Filter 2.** Are there any checkpoints that facilitate early correction of mistakes?
  > Three checkpoints: (1) 90-day checkpoint — already established as the go-forward plan for consulting outreach and revenue. (2) 12-month checkpoint — assess whether consulting engagements are generating genuine deal pipeline intelligence, not just revenue. If not, reassess the model. (3) Ongoing macro cycle monitoring — capital window availability is a live variable; if the window closes (likely within a few years), creative navigation required. Mitigation already in progress: research into downturn-resilient and countercyclical sectors begun at outset, intentionally front-loading resilient sector focus.

- [x] **Filter 3.** Is the path clear and direct?
  > No — and this is named as a root problem. Confidence is high on the technology side (AI transformation, agentic systems, EBITDA improvement). The gap is PE-specific knowledge: deal structuring, sourcing, acquisition mechanics. Action required: daily dedicated study in 2026 — books, podcasts, thought leaders in the PE/turnaround space. Key resources: Boston PE partner (done this at scale, relationship exists but underutilized), Keith Cunningham (turnaround model), macro research already underway. Macro thesis is forming: PE deals from the low-rate era are hitting refinancing pressure; if operations weren't improved, those businesses may return to market at distressed prices — which is a specific acquisition window for an operator-AI transformation buyer. Private credit cracks and sector-wide underperformance could compress capital availability. Sectors with downturn resilience being researched in parallel.

- [x] **Filter 4.** Is there a real or accepted sacrifice to make?
  > Yes — time and learning. Willing to sacrifice significant time traveling, studying, and acquiring new skills: sales, marketing, negotiation, persuasion, PE deal mechanics, deal language, what capital partners want, and how deals are structured. Unwilling to risk own capital — strategy is explicitly OPM (other people's money). Willing to put himself out there personally.

- [x] **Filter 5.** Does the value of the objective reflect the pain of commitment?
  > Yes — for several reasons. Freedom and power to live the life the family wants. Getting the family out of their current situation. Creating an expansive mindset for the next generation — the permanence. Most importantly: his father had high dreams but not the how, not the discipline, not the work. He does not want to be that person. He does not want fantasy thinking. He wants his children to see a father who lived it out — not someone who talked about ideas, but someone who did the hard work, bore the cost, and arrived. He wants to be a guide and wisdom source to his children from a place of earned experience, not good intentions.

- [x] **Filter 6.** Will it provide a definite solution to a definite need?
  > Yes on both sides: (1) Investment side — GIC contact (30 years, senior bonds desk) confirmed directly: "there's always more capital than opportunities." Quality deal flow is the scarce resource. (2) Business side — businesses are the economic engine of every country; the need for AI-enabled operational improvement and turnaround capability is real and growing. Self-assessment: historically weak at validating market needs before building — has made "build it and they will come" mistakes. Key learning: networks, conversations, ear to the ground, constant validation. Prior win that demonstrates pattern recognition: identified MetaTrader 4 as an emerging platform early, pioneered it in Australia, established brokerage referral agreements, and was paid generously for a $1,000 website and forum. Early-mover timing and need identification is a proven capability when applied. Now treating network conversations and market validation as non-negotiable, not optional.

- [x] **Filter 7.** Can you give it your best without reservation?
  > Yes — counting the measured cost, prepared to give his time, talent, effort, and to trade his life for the vision. On the question of hesitation or vacillation as a fear signal: no significant hesitation, because he knows part of the path. You don't always know the exact path, but any part that doesn't work as expected is a learning point, a point for recalibration — not a reason to stop.

- [x] **Filter 8.** Do you have a better plan, and if you do, are you prepared to risk all in it?
  > No better plan at present — aware the plan will unfold as knowledge, awareness, and expertise increase. Believes a good plan deserves all effort and strength; this is a trade of a non-redeemable segment of life. Honest acknowledgment: the plan is currently loose and could be tightened — that's the opportunity, not a reason to hesitate. All in on this one.

- [x] **Filter 9.** Can you look failure in the face and start again with vigor?
  > Yes — failures are stepping stones to success, seeds of greatness. They need to be studied, learned from, and understood: why did this fail? When harnessed properly, failure causes change and growth. They are life lessons, not endpoints.

- [x] **Filter 10.** Are you prepared to out-think your present capacity?
  > Yes — strongly. Peter Daniels calls it prime thinking; Keith Cunningham calls it thinking time (drawn from Napoleon Hill's Think and Grow Rich). The idea of deliberately expanding thinking capacity and generating ideas is something he deeply believes in. Multiple respected mentors have independently converged on this practice. Cunningham recommends 1+ hour/day: empty desk, still, digital connections off, pad (Liam prefers typing — flows faster). Attended Cunningham's 4-Day MBA program in person. Committed to 5 hours/week of structured thinking time as a going-forward practice.

- [x] **Filter 11.** Will you adopt a life commitment to always seek and find your personal destiny with accuracy?
  > Yes — at the heart of this is constantly seeking truth, seeking wisdom, and living in second-level thinking. Howard Marks (whose memos Warren Buffett stops to read) articulates this clearly: first-level thinking processes commonly available facts at face value; second-level thinking goes significantly deeper, finding the connecting tissue between facts, the nodal points, and the deeper cause-and-effect reasoning beneath the surface. A life commitment to seeking destiny with accuracy means operating habitually at that second level.

- [x] **Filter 12.** Will your life create a permanent change or a benefit to someone else?
  > Yes — and this is something pursued with intentionality. One reason for the deep draw to sovereign wealth funds is their intergenerational investment perspective: unlike typical asset classes, they operate without standard redemption cycles and can make investments whose fruit may not be seen for 60 or 100 years — the investment manager may never witness the outcome. The pursuit of permanence connects to the people he most admires: Lee Kuan Yew (founding father of Singapore — steely vision, executed with discipline, whose wisdom inspired world leaders across every domain), Jesus Christ, King Solomon. The common thread is wisdom — true wisdom is unquestionable in its permanence. What he ultimately wants to pursue, boiled all the way down, is wisdom: the kind that shapes nations, outlasts individuals, and leaves something real for generations that follow. That is the permanent change his life is meant to create.
