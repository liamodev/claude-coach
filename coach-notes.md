## Patterns & Observations

**Self-doubt is actually navigational uncertainty.** When asked where self-doubt lives, Liam's answer was precise: "I struggle to know the best next step — is this the best way, is there a better path, how do I get there from here?" This is not capability doubt. It's strategic paralysis — Deliberative + Strategic wiring producing infinite path analysis. The coaching reframe: at the scale he's describing (sovereign funds, governments), no roadmap exists. The path is built by walking. "Good enough next step" is the right filter, not "optimal next step."

*(This section accumulates across all sessions — the most important layer.)*

**The Deliberative trap.** Liam's Deliberative/Analytical wiring shows up as a need to feel "ready" before acting. "The offer doesn't feel ready" was his exact language. The pattern: he waits for certainty that only comes from the action he's avoiding. He knows this intellectually — but that doesn't stop it from showing up. The coaching lever here is to reframe conversations as research, not pitches. That gives the Deliberative brain permission to move.

**Strong strategy, weak implementation cadence.** The Morgan Stanley memo is sharp. The macro research is genuinely impressive (28 pages, primary source). The thinking is there. What's missing is the habit of *showing up and doing it anyway* — outreach, calls, presence. He can think his way to the right answer; he can't think his way to a conversation. This gap is where the work is.

**Authenticity is the superpower, selling is the fear.** He produced the macro structural fragility report for *free* — because it interested him. That's the most honest signal of where his energy goes. The ICP (investment managers/PE) isn't a strategic pivot; it's returning to his natural domain. The coaching challenge is helping him see that the listening/discovery approach *is* selling — just in his language.

**February was contextual, not character.** A miscarriage and 2+ weeks of deep family support is the primary explanation for $0 revenue in February. That was the right call. It would be a coaching mistake to treat February's results as a pattern signal — they're not. The real test is March.

**The bigger destination.** Altitude 7 is a vehicle, not the destination. He's said so himself: the long-term vision involves PE and economic impact on a larger scale. It's worth keeping one eye on this — the day-to-day consulting work and the 20-year vision need to be in conversation with each other.
